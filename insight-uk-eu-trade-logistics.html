UK to EU Trade: What Buyers Should Ask About Lead Times & Paperwork

The practical questions worth asking before you commit to a delivery date.
Back to Insights

Customs rules and trade agreements between the UK and EU shift over time, so this isn't the place to look for a definitive, permanently-accurate breakdown of current regulations. That's what a customs broker or freight forwarder is for. What's worth covering here is the set of practical questions any trade buyer should be asking before committing to a delivery date across the UK/EU border, since the answers affect your planning regardless of exactly how the rules read this year.

Questions worth asking before you commit to a date

  • Who is responsible for customs clearance? Get clarity on whether the supplier handles this, or whether it lands on you at the receiving end, since this changes both your cost and your timeline exposure.
  • What documentation will accompany the shipment? Commercial invoices, certificates of origin, and any product-specific certification should be confirmed upfront, not discovered when a shipment gets held.
  • What's the realistic buffer for customs delays? A supplier's quoted lead time and the date stock actually clears customs are not always the same thing. Ask what buffer they build in, and build your own downstream commitments around that, not the optimistic number.
  • Are there product-specific import requirements? Certain categories (electricals, cosmetics, food-adjacent products) can carry additional compliance requirements on either side of the border. Confirm this applies to your specific product, not just the general rule.
  • What happens if a shipment is delayed or held? Understand who communicates with you, and how quickly, if something gets stuck: this is often a better indicator of how well a supplier handles logistics than their quoted lead time ever is.

Why this matters more than the quoted lead time alone

Two suppliers quoting the same lead time can have very different track records once cross-border paperwork is involved. The one who's done this consistently, with clean documentation and a realistic buffer built in, will hit their dates far more reliably than the one who's quoting an optimistic best-case number and hoping nothing goes wrong at the border.
